Can IBS and gut problems be mind-body?

By Leyla Fıratlı · 12 October 2026 · 2 min read

Gut symptoms are a very common way mind-body pain can show up. The gut and the brain are in constant conversation through the nervous system, and many gut symptoms have a strong mind-body component.

The gut listens to the nervous system

You already know this from your own life. Your stomach drops when you get bad news. It churns before a hard conversation. We talk about "gut feelings" for a reason.

The gut even has its own nervous system, a vast web of nerve cells lining the digestive tract, always sending messages to the brain and receiving them back. Gut specialists now describe IBS as a disorder of gut-brain interaction. In other words, the gut can look normal on every test and still be in real distress because of the signals it's getting.

How do I know if my gut problems might be mind-body?

Some signs I look for:

  • It follows your life. It flares when you're under pressure, and settles when you're away or at ease.
  • It started during a hard time in your life.
  • The foods that set it off keep changing.
  • You have other symptoms that come and go, like headaches or back pain.

Working with the gut

We use slow breath to help your body come out of alert. We practice staying with the sensations in your belly, without bracing against them.

We also look gently at the fear that often grows around gut problems: fear of certain foods, fear of being far from a bathroom, fear of the next flare. That fear makes sense, and it also keeps the alarm going. As it softens, the gut often has more room to settle too.

Related questions

Why does my gut flare when I'm stressed?

When your nervous system is on alert, digestion is one of the first things it changes. The gut can speed up or slow down and become more sensitive, and you might feel that as cramping or urgency.

What is the gut-brain connection?

It's the two-way communication between your digestive system and your brain, through nerves and chemical messengers. What happens in your mind affects your gut, and what happens in your gut affects your mind.

Can anxiety cause IBS symptoms?

Anxiety and IBS often go together. An anxious nervous system can make the gut more sensitive and reactive, and gut symptoms can in turn make anxiety worse.

Is IBS all in my head?

No. IBS symptoms are real and physical. What's different is that they come from how the gut and nervous system communicate, rather than from damage you can see on a test.
