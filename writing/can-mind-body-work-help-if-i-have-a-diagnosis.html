Can mind-body work help if I have a diagnosis like a herniated disc or scoliosis?

By Leyla Fıratlı · 12 October 2026 · 3 min read

Yes, it often can. Mind-body work doesn't deny what's on your scan, but many people with the very same findings live without pain. What matters is the role your nervous system is playing in amplifying, or even creating, your symptoms.

Can you have a herniated disc and no pain?

Yes, and it's far more common than most people think. A finding on a scan describes the structure of your body. Pain is an experience your nervous system creates. Two people with the same scan can feel completely different, and the same person can feel very different from one week to the next with no change in the scan at all.

A 2015 review looked at the spines of more than 3,000 people with no back pain. Signs of disc degeneration showed up in 37% of 20-year-olds and 96% of 80-year-olds. Changes like these are simply part of having a spine and living in a body over time.

My scoliosis

At 12, I was diagnosed with advanced scoliosis. Doctors told me I would either live in pain for the rest of my life or have surgery to rearrange my spine. For years after that, I believed my body was fragile, something to endure rather than trust.

I think that belief hurt me as much as anything in my spine did. I grew up afraid of my own body, and fear keeps the nervous system on alert.

When I started doing mind-body work for my lower back pain, the scoliosis pain I've had my whole life also lifted magically. I started looking at nature and noticed that so much of nature is imperfect. Most tree spines are curved. I started seeing my scoliosis as a natural way I have evolved, instead of a pathology that needs fixing.

How do we work with a diagnosis?

We don't argue with your scan. We get curious about the pain.

When is it louder, and when is it quieter? What does it do when you feel safe, and what does it do when you're under pressure? Does it change when you're absorbed in something you love?

If the pain shifts with these things, your nervous system is involved, and that's the part we can work with. Your spine may look the way it looks. Your relationship with it can still change.

Related questions

Which conditions often have a mind-body component?

Many people are told their pain comes from a herniated disc, arthritis or scoliosis, yet these findings are also common in people with no pain at all. Conditions like fibromyalgia, irritable bowel syndrome, migraines, tension headaches, long COVID, pelvic pain and repetitive strain often involve the nervous system in a big way. Some people with autoimmune conditions also find their pain is amplified by the nervous system.

Is scoliosis always painful?

No. Many people with scoliosis live with little or no pain.

Should I ignore what my doctor says?

No. Keep your doctor. We will work alongside them.

Can mind-body work help if I've already had surgery?

Often, yes. Some people still have pain after surgery even when the operation went well. When that happens, the nervous system may be holding on to the alarm, and that's something we can work with.
