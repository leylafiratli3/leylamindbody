What is conscious connected breathwork?

By Leyla Fıratlı · 12 October 2026 · 3 min read

Conscious connected breathwork is a way of breathing with no pause between the inhale and the exhale, kept up for a sustained stretch of time, with the guidance of a certified practitioner.

How is it different from slow breathing?

Slow breathing calms the nervous system down. Connected breathing does something else. It brings energy and sensation up, and with them, the feelings you've been holding down.

Both have their place, and I use both. Some days your body needs to settle. Other days it's ready to feel.

Where it comes from

Long before breath was studied in labs, it was held as sacred. People across the world have worked with the breath for centuries, as medicine, as prayer, and as a way back to the body and to something greater than ourselves. In Arabic, nafas (breath) and nafs (soul) share a root. The breath and the spirit are one.

Conscious connected breathwork, as it's practiced today, took shape only in the last few decades. But it stands on this much older ground. When I guide breath, I try to honor that it doesn't belong to any one method or teacher. It belongs to all of us, and our ancestors have been breathing this way toward feeling for a very long time.

What does it feel like?

Everyone is different, and the same person can have very different sessions. These are some common experiences:

  • tingling in your hands, or around your mouth
  • feeling hot or cold
  • cramping in your hands, which passes when your breathing returns to normal
  • waves of emotion, like crying, anger, laughter, or all of them at once
  • memories or images rising up
  • a deep quiet afterward
  • nothing at all

Why I use it with chronic pain

Breath is the bridge between your conscious mind and your autonomic nervous system, and conscious connected breathwork is a beautiful way to walk across that bridge together.

When pain is protecting you from something you haven't been able to feel, the breath can give that feeling a way to move. I wrote more about this in What does breath have to do with chronic pain?

Is breathwork safe for everyone?

Connected breathing can be intense for the body, and it isn't right for everyone. We talk about your medical history before bringing any breathwork into our practice. If you have any contraindications, we discuss them and adjust the practice accordingly.

What happens in a session?

We begin by arriving: grounding, settling in, and making sure you feel safe enough to breathe. Then I guide you into the breath, and support you to stay with whatever comes up in your body. Afterward, we take time to rest and reflect, and I give you simple tools to use between sessions.

We move at your pace. You can slow down or stop at any point.

Related questions

Why do my hands tingle or cramp during breathwork?

Breathing faster than usual lowers the carbon dioxide in your blood for a while, and that can cause tingling and cramping in your hands and face. It's temporary, and it eases when you slow your breath.

Is conscious connected breathwork the same as holotropic breathwork?

They're related but different. Holotropic breathwork, developed by Stanislav and Christina Grof, usually involves longer sessions with loud, evocative music. Conscious connected breathwork is a broader term for continuous breathing without pauses, and styles vary a lot.

Is it the same as the Wim Hof method?

No. The Wim Hof method uses rounds of fast breathing followed by long breath holds. Conscious connected breathwork keeps the breath flowing continuously, without holds.

How long is a breathwork session?

All my sessions are breather-led and their length varies based on the needs of the breather.

Can I do breathwork online?

Yes. All my sessions are online, so you can work with me from anywhere.
