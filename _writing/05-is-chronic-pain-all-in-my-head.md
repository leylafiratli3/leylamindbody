No. Mind-body pain is not imagined. It's a real, physical experience. What's different is where it comes from: a nervous system stuck on alert, rather than damage in the tissue.

## Why does "it's in your head" feel like an insult?

When someone says your pain might be connected to stress or emotions, it can sound like they're saying you made it up, or that you're weak. Many of the people I work with have already been dismissed by doctors, especially women, people of color and queer and trans folks, whose pain has been doubted for generations.

That is not what I'm saying. Your pain is real. I know that.

## Real pain without damage

Think about blushing when you're embarrassed, or your stomach dropping before a hard conversation. These are physical events in the body, and they start with an emotion. Nobody calls them imaginary.

Chronic pain can work in a similar way. The nervous system senses danger and sounds an alarm in the body. The alarm is real. You feel it in your back or your gut, and no amount of telling yourself to relax makes it stop.

In a way, all pain is "in your head," because all pain is created by the brain, even the pain of a broken bone. That doesn't make any of it less real.

## Can the nervous system unlearn pain?

Yes, and this is why I find this work so hopeful. If your pain came only from damage that couldn't be repaired, there wouldn't be much you could do. But a nervous system that has learned to stay on alert can learn something else. Through breath, through somatic awareness and through slowly coming to feel safe in your body again, the alarm can quiet down.

Your body isn't broken. It's paying attention.

## Related questions

### Does this mean the pain is my fault?

No. You didn't choose this, and you couldn't have thought your way out of it. Your nervous system has been protecting you the best way it knows.

### Can stress really cause physical pain?

Yes. Stress and strong emotions change how tense your muscles are, how you breathe and how sensitive your nervous system is to pain signals. When stress goes on for a long time, pain can stay even with no injury.

### Why do doctors sometimes say pain is "in my head"?

Usually because tests don't show a clear cause, and many doctors aren't trained in how the nervous system creates pain. Without that knowledge, "it's in your head" can come out sounding like "it's not real," which isn't true.
