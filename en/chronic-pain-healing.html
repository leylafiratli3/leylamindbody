Work with me

Freedom from chronic pain

One way of working together, shaped around you: 1:1 mind-body sessions for pain that lingers, flares, or doesn't make sense, so you can move, rest, and live without fear of your own body.

We bring Dr. John Sarno's mind-body approach, breathwork, and somatic awareness, held with a decolonial lens, into a single path. No menu of services to choose between; just the work that helps you find your freedom.

Book a free intro call

If you've ever had a headache after working too much, or a stomach ache when you felt anxious, you already know your emotional state can create physical sensations. The brain can alter muscle tension, blood flow, and neural signaling in ways that create very real pain.

In a world where none of us feels truly safe, your nervous system can stay locked in threat-response. Emotions like rage, grief, fear, and shame start to feel dangerous, so they get pushed out of awareness and deeper into the body.

When that pressure builds beyond what you can contain, the nervous system steps in. It creates physical pain strong enough to pull your attention away from feelings that seem too overwhelming or unsafe to experience. Pain becomes a distraction from emotion, a shield against the systems you're navigating, and a boundary your body sets when you cannot.

Our work together begins with helping you deeply believe that you have the power to live free of pain. From there, we retrain your nervous system to recognize that pain is no longer a necessary form of protection. As we create space, safety, and compassion around your emotions and the situation that created the pain, it can recede, because it no longer serves a protective purpose.

This work is for you if…

  • You've been living with pain that persists over time, without a clear cause or explanation.
  • Your pain lingers, flares, or shifts without warning.
  • Your scans or tests look "normal," but the symptoms remain.
  • You feel drained by endless treatments, appointments, and quick solutions that don't last.
  • You've begun to fear your own body, or movement itself.
  • You sense that emotions are connected to your pain, but don't know where to start.

Understanding pain as communication from your nervous system is the first step to reconnection. When you listen instead of fear, and respond instead of resist, the cycle can slowly begin to shift.

Questions you might have

So… is this pain all in my head?

No. The pain is not imagined, it is a real, physical experience, but its origin is in the nervous system, not in structural damage.

Do I need perfect scans or a doctor's clearance first?

It can be helpful to have a doctor rule out anything serious, that peace of mind often supports the process. That said, this work is safe, educational, and has no contraindications, so you're welcome to begin whether or not you choose to seek further medical tests.

What if I have a real diagnosis, like herniated disc, scoliosis, arthritis, or IBS?

Mind-body work does not deny these findings, but research and experience show that many people with the same diagnoses live pain-free. What matters is the role your nervous system is playing in amplifying or even creating the symptoms.

How many sessions will it take?

Everyone's journey is unique. Some people feel relief after a single insight; others take weeks or months of steady practice. I usually recommend 4 to 6 sessions to build a strong foundation.

What if I don't get better?

This is one of the most common fears. This work requires persistence, and setbacks are part of the process. I'm confident in the tools, but your nervous system may resist at first. The good news: countless people around the world, myself included, have found freedom this way, and I believe you can too.

What is mind-body pain commonly mistaken for?

Mind-body pain is often mistaken for medical or structural conditions such as herniated or bulging discs, scoliosis, arthritis or joint degeneration, fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue, irritable bowel syndrome and other gut issues, migraines or tension headaches, pelvic pain syndromes, carpal tunnel or repetitive strain injuries, autoimmune-like flare-ups, and many more.
