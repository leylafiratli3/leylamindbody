What does breath have to do with chronic pain?

By Leyla Fıratlı · 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

Conscious connected breathwork is a practice of feeling: a way into the emotions and nervous system patterns we usually keep out of awareness. Much chronic pain comes from a nervous system that treats certain emotions as dangerous. Breathwork gives the body a chance to feel those emotions safely, so it no longer needs pain to protect you from them.

Why emotions turn into pain

Most of us learned early on that some feelings weren't safe. Maybe anger got you punished. Maybe grief made the adults around you uncomfortable. Maybe there was simply no room for what you felt, so you learned to push it down and keep going.

The nervous system remembers. It decides that these emotions are a threat, and it works hard to keep them out of awareness. Dr. John Sarno believed pain is one of the ways it does this: a loud physical signal that pulls your attention away from what feels too dangerous to feel.

This happens beneath conscious thought. You can understand it all in your head and still have the pain, because the pattern lives in the body.

What happens in a breathwork session?

We start by talking: about your pain, your life, and what you'd like to explore. Then you lie down, and I guide you into the breath.

As you breathe, I support you to stay with whatever arises, without judging it or pushing it away. Sometimes that's an emotion you haven't let yourself feel in years. Sometimes it's sensation in the very place that hurts. Sometimes it's quiet.

After the breath, we take time to rest and talk about what came up, so it can settle and make sense.

How breathwork can change the pattern

When you let yourself feel an emotion your nervous system has been guarding against, and nothing terrible happens, your body learns something new. This feeling is survivable. I am safe enough to feel it.

This isn't about forcing a big release in one session. It's about repetition: feeling a little more each time, in a space that is held and safe. Over time, the nervous system starts to update its old map. When the emotions it was protecting you from are no longer seen as dangerous, the pain has less reason to stay.

Learning to stay

Most of us spend our lives running from pain. We distract ourselves, we push through, we wait for it to pass. It's a natural instinct, but it keeps the nervous system convinced that what we're running from is dangerous. The breath teaches us to stay. To turn toward the pain and actually look at it.

The breath is the vehicle. It brings you into your body, and it keeps you there when everything in you wants to leave. One breath at a time, you stay a little longer than you normally would.

And when you stay, you start to see what's really there. Often the pain softens, or changes shape, or shows you the feeling it's been covering. The nervous system learns that it can be with this, and it doesn't have to run anymore.

Something to try now

Sit or lie somewhere comfortable. Place a hand where your pain lives. Breathe slowly into that place, softly, with no need to change anything.

Then ask quietly: if this pain had a voice, what would it say?

You don't need an answer. Just notice what comes: a word, an image, an emotion, or nothing at all. Noticing is the practice.

Related questions

What does conscious connected breathwork feel like?

It's different for everyone. Many people feel tingling in their hands or face, waves of emotion, or a sense of being deeply in their body, and some people feel nothing at all. We stay open to all possibilities.

Is it normal to cry during breathwork?

Yes. Tears, laughter or shaking can happen, but they are not the goal.

Can breathwork make my pain worse?

Sometimes, for a short while. Turning toward pain and emotion can stir things up before they settle. This is why we go slowly and take time to integrate after each session.

Is breathwork enough by itself?

For me, breath is one part. We also learn how pain works and listen to what the pain is protecting you from.

Do I need experience with breathwork?

No. We start wherever you are.
